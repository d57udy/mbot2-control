# Pin heading to walls, then freeze the map

The mBot2's ~30 cm home error and its repeated collisions with a known door are two failures with one root cause each, and both are fixable in browser JavaScript without exotic algorithms. The home error is rotational: a heading drift of about 1.5°/min while driving at ~10 cm/s for 2 minutes produces roughly 31 cm of lateral error by simple geometry, which matches the field observation almost exactly. The door collisions come from the order of operations: a sweep whose match was rejected is still written into the map at the drifted pose, which paints a second copy of the doorframe and wall, and that duplicate then competes with the real structure in every later match. The best documented answer for a single wide-beam sonar is a layered one. First, bound heading with gyro bias re-estimation at every stop, per-segment gyro/odometry arbitration (Borenstein's Gyrodometry), and a Manhattan-world heading snap from the wall-normal "regions of constant depth" visible in each 360° sweep. Second, never write a rejected sweep into the persistent map, build the matching likelihood field only from confirmed cells, size the search window from predicted uncertainty, and replace best-vs-runner-up with a score-surface covariance test. Third, keep sweeps as keyframes so a later loop closure can rebuild the grid. A particle filter (MCL) with a likelihood-field sonar model is a strong optional fourth layer and costs only milliseconds per sweep. Published analogues (LEGO EV3 sonar MCL) suggest **3 to 10 cm** position accuracy is realistic once localized in a known room; nobody has published measured accuracy for a single HC-SR04 at room scale, so the mBot2 numbers below are targets to verify, not guarantees.

## Rotational drift explains the 30 cm, and the map loop explains the door

Start with the system as it stands. The robot keeps a 5 cm occupancy grid with per-cell hit/miss evidence, dead-reckons from wheel encoders plus an integer-degree gyro, and matches each 360° sweep (resampled to 5° bins, roughly 72 bins from 70 to 85 raw readings) against a likelihood field using a correlative search over ±40 cm and ±20°, accepting a match when the best score clearly beats the runner-up. A start-scan anchor supports a home check. Crucially, it integrates every sweep into the map, including sweeps whose match was rejected.

The geometry of heading error is unforgiving. A constant heading offset Δθ over a straight distance d gives a lateral error of d·sin Δθ, so 1° costs about 1.75 cm per metre travelled. When heading drifts at a constant rate ω while driving at speed v for time T, lateral error grows quadratically as roughly v·ω·T²/2. At 10 cm/s and 1.5°/min for 120 s, that is **about 31 cm**, essentially the observed home error (this is a calculation, and the 10 cm/s speed is assumed). Borenstein and Feng put the underlying point bluntly: orientation errors "grow without bound into lateral position errors" ([Borenstein & Feng 1996](https://johnloomis.org/ece445/topics/odometry/borenstein/paper58.pdf)). Floor transitions make it worse on a small robot. When one wheel climbs a threshold it must travel an extra distance ΔD, leaving a permanent heading error of about ΔD/b, where b is the wheelbase; 6 to 12 mm cables left **0.5 to 0.8°** per bump on a LabMate with a 340 mm wheelbase ([Borenstein & Feng 1996, Gyrodometry](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~motionplanning/papers/sbp_papers/integrated2/borenstien_gyro.pdf)). With an mBot2 wheelbase of roughly 12 to 13 cm (not verified), the same physics yields close to 1° per transition, nearly three times the LabMate's sensitivity. To keep home error under ~5 cm on a 12 m excursion, average heading error must stay below about 0.5 to 1°, which implies an environmental heading fix every 1 to 3 m or 30 to 60 s, plus one after every detected transition.

The door collisions follow from the map update policy. Every mainstream grid SLAM system matches before inserting and inserts only at the corrected pose: Cartographer optimizes the scan pose against the current submap "prior to inserting a scan into a submap" ([Hess et al. 2016](https://research.google.com/pubs/archive/45466.pdf)), and slam_toolbox's localization mode keeps recent scans in a rolling buffer so "the underlying map is not affected" ([slam_toolbox README](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox)). GMapping falls back to the motion model when scan matching fails and still updates the map ([Grisetti et al. 2007](http://www2.informatik.uni-freiburg.de/~stachnis/pdf/grisetti07tro.pdf)), which is exactly the behaviour that reproduces a duplicated doorframe under long dropouts. Three compounding effects are likely at work in the mBot2. First, a doorway sweep is dominated by two parallel walls, so the score surface is a ridge along the wall direction; the runner-up sits one or two cells away or one door width away and scores almost the same, so best-vs-runner-up rejects matches whose heading and cross-wall position are actually well determined. Second, a ±40 cm window is too small once heading has drifted for a few metres, because a 10° heading error alone displaces a 1.5 m sonar endpoint by about 26 cm. Third, the rejected sweep is then integrated at the drifted pose, creating phantom walls that make the next match even more ambiguous. The planner then treats the real door and wall as free space beside a phantom copy, which is how the robot ends up colliding with structure it has already mapped. This diagnosis is inferred from the literature and the reported symptoms; it should be confirmed by logging matches and rejections on a replay of the failing run.

## Correlative matching and MCL fit sparse sonar; ICP and GMapping do not

A single HC-SR04 violates the assumptions of most laser-era methods. Each return's bearing is uncertain by roughly half the 25 to 30° beam width, oblique walls drop out entirely, and a sweep yields only 70 to 85 readings. The research record sorts the candidate methods fairly cleanly.

| Approach | Evidence with sparse or wide-beam sensing | Fit for mBot2 |
|---|---|---|
| Correlative scan-to-map matching (Olson) | Posterior error "independent of the initial error" up to 74° and 3 m; ICP quality "completely unacceptable" at high uncertainty ([Olson 2009](https://april.eecs.umich.edu/pdfs/olson2009icra.pdf)). Score surfaces get flat and ambiguous with sparse data ([Zhou et al. 2022](https://www.sihangliu.com/docs/iros2022.pdf)) | Best deterministic core; needs better confidence and window logic |
| MCL with likelihood-field model | Original MCL used 7 to 24 sonars, 1,000 to 5,000 samples optimal in real time ([Fox et al. 1999](http://robots.stanford.edu/papers/fox.aaai99.pdf)); KLD-sampling cut sonar global localization from ~50,000 to ~3,000 samples ([Fox, KLD-sampling](http://papers.neurips.cc/paper/1998-kld-sampling-adaptive-particle-filters.pdf)) | Strong optional layer; handles multi-modal ambiguity |
| RCD / Hough line and corner extraction | RCDs are "the most natural and reliable features contained in sonar scans" ([Leonard & Durrant-Whyte](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/180885)); Hough grouping robust to specular and phantom readings ([Yap & Shelton 2009](https://www.cs.ucr.edu/~cshelton/papers/docs/ICRA2009.pdf)) | Ideal for heading and wall-distance fixes from each sweep |
| Elfes / MURIEL sonar occupancy models | Cone-shaped free evidence, thin occupied arc, specular readings near max range discarded ([Moravec & Elfes 1985](https://frc.ri.cmu.edu/~hpm/project.archive/robot.papers/1985/al2.html)); MURIEL pose buckets stop redundant readings overcounting ([Konolige 1997](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1008806422571)) | Use for the mapping update, not for localization |
| ICP / PL-ICP | PL-ICP is precise but "less robust to very large initial displacement errors" ([Censi 2008](https://censi.science/pub/research/2008-icra-plicp.pdf)); bearing smear biases point correspondences | Poor fit |
| GMapping / grid RBPF | Fails below ~30 beams per scan; Hector and Cartographer produce nothing meaningful below ~60 ([Zhou et al. 2022](https://www.sihangliu.com/docs/iros2022.pdf)); grid RBPF on sonar smears and needs per-environment tuning ([Yap & Shelton 2009](https://www.cs.ucr.edu/~cshelton/papers/docs/ICRA2009.pdf)) | Not recommended |
| Line-landmark graph SLAM with Manhattan prior | 11-beam graph SLAM reached 4.6 to 8.5 cm mean translational error on standard datasets at ~0.8 ms per update ([Zhou et al. 2022](https://www.sihangliu.com/docs/iros2022.pdf)); soft Manhattan constraints cut error further with 4 beams ([Han et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.15736)) | Best full-SLAM option, but overkill for one known room |

Two patterns stand out. Every successful sparse-sensing SLAM system groups several sparse scans into a "multiscan" before extracting structure ([Beevers & Huang 2006](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/1642043)), and every one leans on an orthogonality prior; Yap and Shelton report that without it "the resulting maps and robot trajectories are not properly estimated and corrected" ([Yap & Shelton 2009](https://www.cs.ucr.edu/~cshelton/papers/docs/ICRA2009.pdf)). The mBot2's in-place 360° sweep is already a multiscan taken from a single position, with only gyro-bounded rotational error inside it, which is a more favourable situation than the moving multiscans in those papers. The caveat from Beevers is that spinning in place "dramatically increases the pose uncertainty and decreases the scan density" ([Beevers & Huang 2006 PDF](http://cs.krisbeevers.com/research/slam_icra06.pdf)), so sweep-internal heading must come from the gyro, not wheel odometry.

The sensor model matters as much as the search method. A likelihood-field model (project each beam endpoint, look up distance to the nearest occupied cell) is cheap and smooth, but it ignores the cone. The sonar-specific remedies are well documented: exclude max-range or timeout readings from weighting, as Yap and Shelton did with a 50 mm range sigma ([Yap & Shelton 2009](https://www.cs.ucr.edu/~cshelton/papers/docs/ICRA2009.pdf)); keep a uniform floor so outliers cannot dominate, the way Nav2's AMCL defaults mix z_hit 0.5 and z_rand 0.5 with sigma_hit 0.2 m ([nav2_params.yaml](https://github.com/ros-navigation/navigation2/blob/main/nav2_bringup/params/nav2_params.yaml)); and recognise that the incidence cutoff varies hugely with surface, from 81° on rough stone to only **16° on a flat wooden panel** at 1 m ([Burguera et al. 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3267219/)). Parquet-adjacent furniture and painted doors will behave like the wooden panel, so most oblique readings are absent, not wrong, and must not be treated as free space.

## Three cheap mechanisms bound heading below one degree

Heading needs three independent corrections, each addressing a different error source.

**Gyro bias at standstill.** MEMS gyros in the CyberPi's class drift even after calibration. The MPU-6886 datasheet (a representative ESP32-board IMU; Makeblock does not confirm which chip CyberPi uses) lists bias variation with temperature of typically ±0.01 °/s per °C ([InvenSense MPU-6886](https://m5stack.oss-cn-shenzhen.aliyuncs.com/resource/docs/datasheet/core/MPU-6886-000193%2Bv1.1_GHIC_en.pdf)), so a 5 °C warm-up from motors and the ESP32 shifts bias by about 3°/min, the same order as the observed drift. A small differential robot with an MPU6050 needed 23 s static before drift fell below 0.5°/min and drifted about 2°/min while moving ([Garcia-Saura 2015](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1509.02154)). Robot cleaners solve this by stopping, averaging gyro output to get a new offset, and correcting accumulated heading ([KR100486505B1](https://patents.google.com/patent/KR100486505B1/en)). On the mBot2 the integer-degree yaw makes direct rate estimation coarse: at 1.5°/min, one count takes about 40 s to appear. The practical rule is therefore twofold: freeze heading whenever both encoder speeds are zero (any yaw change during a stop is pure bias), and estimate a bias rate from stops of 10 s or longer, smoothed across stops with an exponential moving average (α about 0.2 to 0.3).

**Gyro versus odometry arbitration.** Borenstein's Gyrodometry trusts odometry by default and switches to the gyro only when the two disagree sharply, because bumps and slips act briefly. In a 140 s run over 15 bumps, odometry accumulated −9°, the gyro about 4.2°, and Gyrodometry stayed "well under ±0.5°", roughly 18 times better than odometry and 8 times better than the gyro alone ([Borenstein & Feng 1996](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~motionplanning/papers/sbp_papers/integrated2/borenstien_gyro.pdf)). Their per-sample threshold of 1.25°/s cannot be applied directly to a 1° yaw quantum read every 120 ms (one count is ~8°/s), so the mBot2 version must work per segment: over each straight leg or turn, compare Δyaw from the gyro with (dR − dL)/b from the encoders, use the gyro if they disagree by more than about 2°, otherwise use odometry or a weighted blend. Because the yaw is integrated on the board, differencing absolute readings over a segment carries only ±0.5° quantization error that does not accumulate. iRobot detects carpet drift the same way, from the difference between odometry and gyro heading change ([US9223312B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9223312B2/en)). A flagged disagreement, or a pitch/roll transient from the accelerometer, marks a probable floor transition and should trigger an immediate wall-heading check. During in-place sweeps on carpet, odometry rotation is unreliable because of side slip and castor drag, so sweep angles should come from the gyro.

**Manhattan wall heading from each sweep.** This is the correction that actually bounds drift. Indoor walls are mostly parallel or perpendicular, so heading can be re-anchored modulo 90°. In a rotating sonar sweep, a flat wall returns only when the beam axis is near the wall normal, producing a plateau of near-constant range about one beam width wide (about 6 readings at the mBot2's ~4.5° raw spacing). Leonard and Durrant-Whyte's RCD theory says the plateau's centre bearing is the wall normal ([Leonard 1992](http://web.cecs.pdx.edu/~mperkows/temp/KALMAN/KALMAN-PAPERS/sonar-kalman-ms%20thesis%20-%20kluwer1992.pdf)). Heading error is then the wrap of (heading + plateau bearing) to the nearest multiple of 90°, using the median over detected walls. iRobot's camera-free cleaners apply the same rectilinear assumption and only correct when a candidate wall is within **10°** of a known landmark orientation ([US11662743B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11662743B2/en)); SoMaSLAM uses a soft version that still tolerates diagonal walls ([Han et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.15736)). Gate the correction on a magnitude under 10 to 15°, at least two walls agreeing within ~3°, and a plateau width consistent with the beam (which rejects corners and clutter), then apply it with a gain of 0.5 to 1 per sweep. Borenstein's sideways wall-following sonars achieved about ±0.25° as a reference; no published number exists for a single HC-SR04 sweep, and a few degrees per wall, improving with averaging, is a reasonable expectation to verify. The angle-histogram method of Röfer, folding neighbour-segment orientations modulo 90° and finding the peak, is a fallback, though wide-beam smearing biases raw point lines ([Röfer 2002](https://www.informatik.uni-bremen.de/kogrob/papers/iros02.pdf)).

**Systematic calibration** is a one-time multiplier on all of the above. UMBmark's bidirectional square test separates wheelbase error from wheel-diameter mismatch and improved systematic odometry error by **15 to 21 times** (317 mm down to 21 mm) ([Borenstein & Feng 1996](https://johnloomis.org/ece445/topics/odometry/borenstein/paper58.pdf)). On the mBot2 a scaled-down 1 to 1.5 m square works, or more simply an effective wheelbase from N in-place turns (b_eff = b_nominal × θ_odo/θ_gyro), measured separately on parquet and carpet. Makeblock's own 720° calibration routine addresses turn accuracy from part tolerances and persists on the board ([Makeblock support](https://support.makeblock.com/hc/en-us/articles/15962163498903-23-mBot2-Won-t-Turn-Exactly-as-Expected)).

## Stop mapping the doorframe twice: gate, widen, then trust only confirmed cells

The data-association problem has six well-understood fixes, and they should be applied in this order of impact.

**Never write a rejected sweep.** The state machine should be: predict pose and grow uncertainty; match; on acceptance correct pose and then integrate at the corrected pose; on rejection write no hits to the persistent grid. Store the sweep with its uncorrected pose in a pending buffer and retry it after the next accepted match. Over areas already mapped, switch to a frozen-map localization mode, as slam_toolbox and AMCL do ([slam_toolbox README](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox)). A simple automatic switch is to freeze when most predicted endpoints land on already confirmed cells.

**Size the window from uncertainty.** Olson's matcher "scales with the volume of the uncertainty" and his multi-resolution version handled 4 m and 90° errors at over 10 Hz using a max-pooled coarse grid that guarantees no maxima are missed ([Olson 2009](https://april.eecs.umich.edu/pdfs/olson2009icra.pdf)). Cartographer's step rule, δθ = arccos(1 − r²/(2·d_max²)), gives about **1.9°** for r = 5 cm and d_max = 1.5 m ([Hess et al. 2016](https://research.google.com/pubs/archive/45466.pdf)), so heading should be searched at ~2° steps even though sweep bins are 5°. Window = clamp(3σ, minimum 15 cm / 10°, maximum ~80 cm / 45°), where σ_θ grows with time at the measured drift rate and σ_xy grows with distance times slip fraction plus distance times sin σ_θ; shrink back after each accepted match. On rejection, retry once with a wider window, following Karto's response expansion, which widens the angular window by 20° up to three times ([Karto Mapper.cpp](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/blob/ros2/lib/karto_sdk/src/Mapper.cpp)).

**Add the odometry prior to the score.** Karto multiplies each candidate's response by distance and angle penalties floored at 0.5 and 0.9 respectively ([slam_toolbox params](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/blob/ros2/config/mapper_params_online_async.yaml)). That prevents a wider window from jumping to a look-alike one door width away, and it should be applied before any confidence calculation.

**Replace best-vs-runner-up with a covariance test.** Olson computes covariance directly from the score surface over all evaluated poses, which captures both sensor noise and data-association ambiguity; a corridor "provides only a few longitudinal constraints, yielding an elongated uncertainty ellipse" ([Olson 2009](https://april.eecs.umich.edu/pdfs/olson2009icra.pdf)). Karto similarly takes a response-weighted second moment over cells within 0.1 of the best response and inflates it by 1/bestResponse ([Karto Mapper.cpp](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/blob/ros2/lib/karto_sdk/src/Mapper.cpp)). Accept per axis: if heading and cross-track variance are small, apply those corrections and keep along-track from odometry, the partial solution Zhang, Kaess and Singh use for degenerate directions ([Zhang et al. 2016](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1109/ICRA.2016.7487211)). Keep an ambiguity test, but make it Olson's "local uniqueness" and "global sufficiency" pair ([Olson 2009 RAS](https://april.eecs.umich.edu/media/pdfs/olson2009ras.pdf)): the runner-up must lie outside a 2 to 3 cell and ~5° suppression radius, and only a competing peak inside the plausible prior region can veto the match.

**Score robustly and count valid returns.** Sum max(log p, floor) over valid readings only, excluding readings at or beyond 150 cm and timeouts, and refuse to match with fewer than roughly 20 valid of 72 bins (a starting value to tune). Because consecutive readings share overlapping cones, temper each reading's contribution (raise likelihoods to a power below 1) to avoid overconfidence.

**Treat occupancy evidence as a prior, and keep keyframes.** Build the matching likelihood field only from confirmed cells (for example hit count of at least 2 to 3 from different accepted sweeps and P(occ) above ~0.7), so a duplicate from one bad sweep cannot attract future matches. Use deliberately weak per-sweep evidence; Cartographer's defaults are hit 0.55 and miss 0.49 ([trajectory_builder_2d.lua](https://github.com/cartographer-project/cartographer/blob/master/configuration_files/trajectory_builder_2d.lua)), and sonar's wide beam argues for weaker still, plus MURIEL-style pose buckets so one sweep counts at most once per cell per angular bin. Finally, store each accepted sweep as a keyframe (pose plus endpoints) and match against a local submap of the last 5 to 10 keyframes; Cartographer's rule is that submaps "must be small enough so that the drift inside them is below the resolution" ([Cartographer docs](https://google-cartographer-ros.readthedocs.io/en/latest/algo_walkthrough.html)). When the robot returns near an older keyframe, attempt a strict loop closure, relax a tiny pose graph (robust kernels such as switchable constraints tolerate false closures ([Sünderhauf & Protzel 2012](https://nikosuenderhauf.github.io/assets/papers/IROS12-switchableConstraints.pdf))), and re-rasterize the grid from keyframes. That is the only mechanism that removes duplicate walls retroactively; a fused grid alone cannot be repaired.

## Cheap robots succeed by resetting specific error components, not by clever sonar inference

No commercial product used a single swept sonar for localization. Early Roombas did not localize at all; the Roomba 980 added a ~$0.75 camera for VSLAM ([IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/irobot-brings-visual-mapping-and-navigation-to-the-roomba-980)); the Mint used NorthStar ceiling IR spots and "won't be able to localize itself" without them ([IEEE Spectrum Mint review](http://spectrum.ieee.org/automaton/robotics/home-robots/review-evolution-robotics-mint-sweeper)); and Neato built a sub-$30, 360°, 1° resolution laser instead ([Konolige et al. 2008](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/224318681_A_low-cost_laser_distance_sensor)). That is a clear signal about the ceiling for single-sonar systems.

The most transferable commercial design is iRobot's camera-free landmark localization, which uses only bump sensors, short-range IR, encoders and an IMU. It builds straight-wall (at least 30 cm) and corner landmarks, assumes perpendicular walls, tracks drift as an EKF covariance, and when uncertainty exceeds a threshold performs **active re-localization**: drive to the landmark that corrects the largest error component, perpendicular to a wall for the normal distance, or at about 45° to fix x, y and θ together ([US11662743B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11662743B2/en)). An HC-SR04 aimed squarely at a wall is the mBot2's equivalent of that adjacency sensor, at range rather than contact, and two perpendicular wall normals in one sweep fix the full pose up to room symmetry.

The closest academic analogues set realistic expectations. An EV3 with two ultrasonic sensors ran 500-particle MCL (σ of 10 to 15 cm, minimum of 3 pings, readings over 200 cm discarded, walls needed within ±25° of perpendicular) and achieved **3 to 10 cm** position error, converging only after passing distinctive corners ([ev3-localization](https://github.com/jamesjackson/ev3-localization/blob/master/overview.md)). LEGO NXT ultrasonic MCL claimed 1 to 3 cm, but in simulation only ([Liu & Fan](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/328445467_Mobile_Robot_Localisation_and_Navigation_Using_LEGO_NXT_and_Ultrasonic_Sensor)). Beevers closed a loop on a real 8-bit robot with a single IR sensor and 13 particles ([Beevers thesis](http://cs.krisbeevers.com/thesis/krb_phd_thesis.pdf)), and adding a rectilinearity prior cut required particles from 100 to 20 and from 600 to 40 ([Beevers & Huang WAFR 2006](http://cs.krisbeevers.com/research/constraints_wafr06.pdf)). Hobby HC-SR04 "mapping" projects are almost all radar plots or dead reckoning with no reported accuracy ([MapperBot](https://github.com/saques/MapperBot)), and oblique walls produce wild readings such as ~2300 cm ([Arduino Forum](https://forum.arduino.cc/t/hc-sr04-ultrasonic-sensors-incorrect-readings-when-at-an-angle/559813)).

## Recommended stack, parameters and build order

Compute is not the constraint. A sweep takes about 9 s, Olson's correlative matcher ran a 0.5 m / 20° window in 8.4 ms on 2009 hardware ([Olson 2009](https://april.eecs.umich.edu/pdfs/olson2009icra.pdf)), and an exhaustive search at 5 cm / 2° over ±60 cm / ±45° is about 29,000 poses × 72 points ≈ 2.1 M typed-array lookups, a few milliseconds in V8 by estimate. A local micro-benchmark run for this research (Node 22 on an Apple M4 Max, 80 beams, 160 × 160 grid; one unpublished measurement, expect 2 to 5 times slower on a phone or mid-range laptop) measured about **1.6 ms per 1,000 particles** with a likelihood field, 9.1 ms with single-ray casting and 61 ms with a 7-ray cone model. Running localization in a Web Worker is sufficient; WebAssembly is unnecessary.

The ranking below orders changes by expected impact on the two field failures per unit of effort.

| Rank | Component | Concrete algorithm and starting parameters | Addresses |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Map update gating | Integrate only after an accepted match, at the corrected pose; rejected sweeps go to a pending buffer (retry after next accept); frozen-map localization mode on return legs; weak log-odds updates (~0.55 hit / 0.49 miss equivalent), clamped; pose buckets per sweep | Duplicate doorframe, collisions |
| 2 | Heading pipeline | Freeze yaw while both wheels stopped; bias EMA (α 0.2 to 0.3) from stops ≥10 s; per-segment Gyrodometry with 2° threshold; RCD plateau detection (adjacent readings within ~2 cm, width ~25 to 30°) with Manhattan snap, gate <10 to 15°, ≥2 walls within 3°, gain 0.5 to 1; flag transitions via gyro/odometry disagreement or pitch spike and force a sweep | 30 cm home error |
| 3 | Matcher upgrade | Likelihood field from confirmed cells only, σ ~8 to 10 cm, floor (z_rand-like), exclude ≥150 cm and timeouts; window = clamp(3σ, 15 cm/10°, 80 cm/45°); steps 5 cm / 2°, optional 20 cm max-pooled coarse level; Karto prior penalties floored 0.5 / 0.9; Olson covariance confidence with per-axis acceptance; NMS runner-up outside 2 to 3 cells / 5°; min ~20 valid bins; one widened retry | Rejected correct matches |
| 4 | Keyframes and loop closure | Store accepted sweeps as keyframes; local submap of last 5 to 10; strict loop closure near old keyframes; small Gauss-Newton pose graph with a robust kernel; re-rasterize grid from keyframes | Retroactive map repair |
| 5 | Optional MCL layer | 300 to 1,000 particles for tracking (thousands for re-localization), likelihood-field model with tempered per-reading weights, a few percent random injection, resample when N_eff < N/2; feed the RCD wall normals as a Manhattan heading prior | Ambiguity, home relocalization |
| 6 | Calibration | Effective wheelbase per floor type from in-place turns versus gyro; Makeblock 720° routine; optional scaled UMBmark | Systematic drift |

Not recommended for this robot: ICP or PL-ICP on raw sweep points, GMapping or any grid RBPF, and full line-landmark graph SLAM unless the operating area grows well beyond a few rooms.

Expected accuracy is uncertain because no one has published ground truth for this sensor class at room scale. With components 1 to 3 in place, a reasonable target is heading held within about 1 to 2° and home error of **5 to 10 cm** after 2 minutes, consistent with the EV3 results and with the heading budget derived earlier. Without the wall heading snap, the gyro alone will continue to produce errors on the order of the current 30 cm.

The implementation plan follows the ranking, with a measurable gate at each step. First, add logging of every sweep (raw readings, gyro yaw, encoder ticks, predicted pose, match surface summary, accept/reject reason) so the failing run can be replayed offline in the browser. Second, implement map update gating and confirmed-cell likelihood fields, then replay: the duplicate doorframe should disappear and the planner should route through the real door. Third, implement stationary yaw freezing, per-segment Gyrodometry and the RCD Manhattan snap, and test with a 2 minute loop that crosses the parquet/carpet threshold, measuring home error with tape marks and logging heading corrections per sweep (target: corrections stay under ~2°, home error under 10 cm). Fourth, upgrade the matcher's window sizing, prior penalty and covariance confidence, and track the acceptance rate in the doorway, which should rise sharply without an increase in visible map duplication. Fifth, add keyframes, local submaps and loop closure with grid re-rasterization. Sixth, if ambiguity at home or after a kidnap remains a problem, add the MCL layer in a Web Worker. Calibrate the effective wheelbase per floor type at any point; it is independent and cheap.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the problem. The mBot2 does not need a more sophisticated SLAM algorithm; it needs a heading reference that does not drift and a map that refuses to learn from sweeps it could not place. Walls are that heading reference: a single HC-SR04's weakness, returning only near normal incidence, is precisely what makes its wall-normal plateaus a clean compass modulo 90°. Likewise, the best-vs-runner-up test was rejecting good matches in exactly the places (doorways, parallel walls) where the robot most needed them, and the integrate-anyway policy turned each rejection into permanent map damage.

The open question is quantitative. Heading accuracy from an HC-SR04 wall-normal fit, the real incidence cutoff on the household's surfaces, and the mBot2's effective wheelbase on each floor are all unmeasured in the literature, and each directly sets the achievable home error. Instrumenting the robot and replaying logged runs is therefore the highest-leverage next step, because it turns every parameter above from a literature-derived starting point into a measured one.
