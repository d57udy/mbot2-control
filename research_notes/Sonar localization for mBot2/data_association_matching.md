# Data association and robust scan-to-map matching under pose drift (sparse sonar, occupancy grid)

Context assumed throughout: 5 cm occupancy grid with per-cell hit/miss evidence; pose from odometry+gyro; 360 deg ultrasonic sweep of ~70 readings resampled to 5 deg (~72 bins), beam ~25-30 deg, max ~150 cm, specular dropouts; correlative matching against a likelihood field, window +/-40 cm / +/-20 deg, confidence = best vs runner-up. Field failure: heading drift, match rejected for low confidence, sweep integrated anyway at the drifted pose, doorframe duplicated.

## 1. Order of operations: localize first, then update the map; gating and deferring updates

### Takeaway
Every mainstream grid SLAM system matches a scan before inserting it, and only inserts at the corrected pose. The failure observed (reject match, then integrate anyway at the drifted pose) is exactly the case the literature handles by either falling back to odometry with inflated uncertainty or by not touching the persistent map (localization mode). The safest browser-scale fix is: never write hits into the persistent map from a sweep whose match was rejected; buffer it instead.

### Cited Findings
- Cartographer: "Prior to inserting a scan into a submap, the scan pose is optimized relative to the current local submap using a Ceres-based scan matcher"; scans are "inserted into a submap at the best estimated position, which is assumed to be sufficiently accurate for short periods of time." — [Hess et al. 2016](https://research.google.com/pubs/archive/45466.pdf)
- Cartographer submap design rule: "Submaps must be small enough so that the drift inside them is below the resolution." — [Cartographer tuning docs](https://google-cartographer-ros.readthedocs.io/en/latest/algo_walkthrough.html)
- Cartographer motion filter defaults gate insertion: a scan is only inserted if motion exceeds max_time_seconds = 5, max_distance_meters = 0.2 or max_angle_radians = 1 deg. — [trajectory_builder_2d.lua](https://github.com/cartographer-project/cartographer/blob/master/configuration_files/trajectory_builder_2d.lua); [tuning docs](https://google-cartographer-ros.readthedocs.io/en/latest/algo_walkthrough.html)
- Cartographer default inserter: hit_probability = 0.55, miss_probability = 0.49 (deliberately weak per-scan evidence; odds update `M_new = clamp(odds^-1(odds(M_old) * odds(p_hit)))`). — [trajectory_builder_2d.lua](https://github.com/cartographer-project/cartographer/blob/master/configuration_files/trajectory_builder_2d.lua); [Hess et al. 2016](https://research.google.com/pubs/archive/45466.pdf)
- slam_toolbox localization mode: "Loads existing serialized map ... Maintains a rolling buffer of recent scans in the pose-graph. After expiring from the buffer scans are removed and the underlying map is not affected." scan_buffer_size (default 10) doubles as the localization-mode buffer length. — [slam_toolbox README](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox); [mapper_params_online_async.yaml](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/blob/ros2/config/mapper_params_online_async.yaml)
- slam_toolbox gates processing by motion: minimum_travel_distance 0.5 m, minimum_travel_heading 0.5 rad, minimum_time_interval 0.5 s. — [mapper_params_online_async.yaml](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/blob/ros2/config/mapper_params_online_async.yaml)
- GMapping: "If the scan-matching reports a failure, the pose and the weights are computed according to the motion model" (i.e. odometry fallback). The authors report this rarely happened indoors with lidar, but did occur in open areas where the scanner "mainly reports maximum range readings" and when a person blocked the sensor in a glass corridor. — [Grisetti et al. 2007 (T-RO)](http://www2.informatik.uni-freiburg.de/~stachnis/pdf/grisetti07tro.pdf)
- ROS gmapping minimumScore: "Minimum score for considering the outcome of the scan matching good. Can avoid jumping pose estimates in large open spaces when using laser scanners with limited range (e.g. 5m)." Default 0; a community answer reports scores 0-150 for weak structure, up to 600+, and suggests 50. — [gmapping ROS wiki](https://wiki.ros.org/gmapping); [ROS Answers 189159](https://answers.ros.org/question/189159/bad-maps-produced-by-gmapping-in-simulation-with-feature-poor-environments/)
- AMCL (pure localization, never writes the map) only updates the filter after update_min_d = 0.25 m or update_min_a = 0.2 rad of motion (Nav2 defaults). — [nav2_params.yaml](https://github.com/ros-navigation/navigation2/blob/main/nav2_bringup/params/nav2_params.yaml)

### Inferences
- Recommended state machine for the browser app: (a) predict pose and grow covariance from odometry/gyro; (b) match sweep; (c) if accepted, correct pose, then integrate the sweep at the corrected pose; (d) if rejected, do NOT write hits into the persistent grid. Either discard, or store the sweep with its uncorrected pose in a pending buffer and re-try matching later (after the next accepted match, or with a wider window). Optionally integrate only misses (free space) with very low weight, or skip entirely.
- Add an explicit "localization mode" for return trips over already-mapped areas (map frozen, as slam_toolbox/AMCL do), and "mapping mode" only for frontier exploration. A cheap automatic switch: if more than X% of the predicted sweep endpoints fall on cells that are already confirmed-occupied or confirmed-free, treat the region as known and freeze the map.
- Gate new obstacles separately: a cell that is far (> ~2-3 sigma of the pose uncertainty plus beam footprint) from any existing occupied cell is only "new" if it is seen consistently from at least two accepted, well-localized sweeps. This is the occupancy-grid analogue of map-to-scan data association.
- Because GMapping's own odometry-fallback still updates the map, it would reproduce the observed duplicate-doorframe failure under long dropouts; do not copy that behaviour verbatim.

### Gaps
- No source found quantifying how many rejected sweeps a buffered/deferred update strategy should hold for a sonar robot; buffer length must be tuned empirically.

## 2. Search-window sizing from pose covariance; coarse-to-fine and branch-and-bound matching

### Takeaway
Window size should follow predicted uncertainty, especially heading; the correlative family (Olson 2009) is robust to large initial errors as long as the true pose is inside the window, and multi-resolution max-pooled grids (Olson) or branch-and-bound (Cartographer) make large windows cheap. For a 72-point sonar sweep, an exhaustive search over a window grown to, say, +/-60 cm / +/-45 deg is affordable in JavaScript.

### Cited Findings
- Olson multi-resolution: a 3 cm high-res table and a 30 cm low-res table where each low-res cell is the MAX of the corresponding high-res cells, so "the probabilities computed by the low-resolution map are always at least as large as those in the high-resolution map ... it guarantees that we will not miss maxima." Algorithm: evaluate whole 3D window at low-res; repeatedly expand the best unvisited low-res voxel at high res; stop when the best remaining low-res bound < best high-res score. — [Olson ICRA 2009](https://april.eecs.umich.edu/pdfs/olson2009icra.pdf)
- Olson: loop over theta outermost (rotate points once), then x/y are pure translations; step the translation search at the lookup-table resolution. — [Olson ICRA 2009](https://april.eecs.umich.edu/pdfs/olson2009icra.pdf)
- Olson: correlative matcher posterior error "is independent of the initial error" over tested initial errors up to 74 deg and 3 m, while ICP and hill-climbing degrade; multi-resolution version handles "4 m translational errors with 90 degree rotational errors at over 10Hz"; 0.5 m / 20 deg window took 8.4 ms (multi-res CPU) vs 246 ms brute force (2009 hardware). — [Olson ICRA 2009](https://april.eecs.umich.edu/pdfs/olson2009icra.pdf)
- Olson: "complexity scales with the volume of the uncertainty"; lookup-table resolution: "no advantage to resolutions finer than 1.5cm", error "increases rapidly at resolutions coarser than 6cm", recommends 3 cm (for lidar). — [Olson ICRA 2009](https://april.eecs.umich.edu/pdfs/olson2009icra.pdf)
- Cartographer angular step: choose delta_theta so points at max range move at most one cell: `delta_theta = arccos(1 - r^2 / (2 d_max^2))`. Window `w = ceil(W / step)` in each dimension. — [Hess et al. 2016](https://research.google.com/pubs/archive/45466.pdf)
- Cartographer branch and bound: DFS, children visited in order of upper bound; bound uses precomputed grids where each cell holds the max over a 2^h x 2^h block; a score threshold is the initial best score so poor matches are never returned. Defaults: loop-closure window 7 m / 30 deg, branch_and_bound_depth 7; local real-time correlative matcher (off by default) 0.1 m / 20 deg with translation/rotation_delta_cost_weight 0.1. — [Hess et al. 2016](https://research.google.com/pubs/archive/45466.pdf); [pose_graph.lua](https://github.com/cartographer-project/cartographer/blob/master/configuration_files/pose_graph.lua); [trajectory_builder_2d.lua](https://github.com/cartographer-project/cartographer/blob/master/configuration_files/trajectory_builder_2d.lua)
- Cartographer notes the local Ceres matcher is local only, so "good initial estimates are required"; an IMU "can be used to estimate the rotational component" between matches. — [Hess et al. 2016](https://research.google.com/pubs/archive/45466.pdf)
- Karto/slam_toolbox: coarse search 0.5 m (correlation_search_space_dimension) at 1 cm, angle +/-0.349 rad (20 deg) at 0.0349 rad (2 deg), then a fine pass at +/-0.00349 rad; loop closure search space 8 m at 5 cm. use_response_expansion: if the coarse response is exactly 0, widen the angular window by +20 deg up to three times and retry. — [mapper_params_online_async.yaml](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/blob/ros2/config/mapper_params_online_async.yaml); [Karto Mapper.cpp](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/blob/ros2/lib/karto_sdk/src/Mapper.cpp)
- Olson warns the fitted covariance only covers sampled poses: high-probability areas outside the window are missed, "resulting in an over-confident estimate. Thus, it is important to evaluate ... over large areas." — [Olson ICRA 2009](https://april.eecs.umich.edu/pdfs/olson2009icra.pdf)

### Inferences
- Applying Cartographer's formula to this robot: r = 5 cm, d_max = 1.5 m gives delta_theta = arccos(1 - 0.0025/4.5) ~ 0.033 rad ~ 1.9 deg. So a 1-2 deg angular step is sufficient at 5 cm cells; the 5 deg resampling of the sweep is a separate thing (sweep bins), and the pose search should still step heading at ~2 deg.
- Heading dominates: a 10 deg heading error displaces a 1.5 m endpoint by ~26 cm (1.5 * sin 10 deg), and also displaces the robot's own position estimate over the distance travelled since the heading error appeared. A fixed +/-20 deg window is fine for heading but +/-40 cm can be too small after long legs with a heading bias; the translation window should be sized as ~3 sigma of predicted position, where position sigma grows with distance x heading sigma.
- Suggested window rule (implementable): sigma_theta grows with time/rotation from gyro bias (e.g. measured drift deg/min); sigma_xy grows with distance travelled x (odometry slip fraction) plus distance x sin(sigma_theta). Window = clamp(3 sigma, min 15 cm / 10 deg, max ~80 cm / 45 deg). Shrink back after an accepted match.
- Budget check for JS: exhaustive search at 5 cm / 2 deg over +/-60 cm / +/-45 deg = 25 x 25 x 46 ~ 29k poses x 72 points ~ 2.1M lookups per sweep, which is milliseconds in modern JS with typed arrays. A two-level max-pooled grid (e.g. 20 cm) gives Olson-style speedups if needed.
- Retry on rejection with a wider window (Karto's response expansion idea) before declaring failure, and include the odometry prior as a soft penalty (Karto/Cartographer delta-cost weights) so that the wider window does not jump to a distant look-alike.

### Gaps
- No source gives heading-drift statistics for the mBot2 gyro; window growth constants must be measured.

## 3. Confidence and ambiguity measures; failure modes; avoiding rejection of correct low-margin matches

### Takeaway
Best-vs-runner-up is fragile: the runner-up is often a neighbour of the true peak, or a translated copy along a corridor/door wall, so it rejects correct matches. Better: fit a covariance over the score surface (Olson, Karto), accept a match if its covariance is small in the directions that matter (or accept the constrained directions only), and test "local uniqueness" with non-maximum suppression, not raw runner-up.

### Cited Findings
- Olson covariance from the score surface: treat normalized scores as probabilities over all evaluated poses x_j; `K = sum x_j x_j^T p_j`, `u = sum x_j p_j`, `s = sum p_j`, `Sigma = K/s - u u^T / s^2`. This captures both sensor noise and data-association ambiguity; a long hallway "provides only a few longitudinal constraints, yielding an elongated uncertainty ellipse." — [Olson ICRA 2009](https://april.eecs.umich.edu/pdfs/olson2009icra.pdf)
- Karto covariance: averages all poses tying for best response; positional covariance is a response-weighted second moment over all search cells with response >= best - 0.1; minimum variance 0.1 x (search resolution)^2; covariance multiplied by 1/bestResponse ("increase variance for poorer responses"); angular variance computed similarly in the fine pass. Response clamped to [0,1]. — [Karto Mapper.cpp](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/blob/ros2/lib/karto_sdk/src/Mapper.cpp)
- Karto acceptance thresholds (slam_toolbox defaults): link_match_minimum_response_fine 0.1; loop closure requires coarse response >= 0.35, coarse variance <= 3.0 (loop_match_maximum_variance_coarse), fine response >= 0.45, chain of >= 10 scans. — [mapper_params_online_async.yaml](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/blob/ros2/config/mapper_params_online_async.yaml)
- Cartographer acceptance: constraint min_score 0.55, global_localization_min_score 0.6 (score = mean grid probability at scan points). — [pose_graph.lua](https://github.com/cartographer-project/cartographer/blob/master/configuration_files/pose_graph.lua)
- Cartographer notes wrong matches "may happen in locally symmetric environments, such as office cubicles" and uses a Huber loss in the pose graph to limit their effect. — [Hess et al. 2016](https://research.google.com/pubs/archive/45466.pdf)
- Olson place recognition: two tests. "Local uniqueness" ensures "there is no similar loop closure nearby ... if a hypothesis passes, it is either correct or significantly wrong" (addresses the "picket fence" problem where translating by one post gives another good match). "Global sufficiency" ensures the match is "large enough to be unambiguous with respect to the positional uncertainty": "if the robot knows about where it is, very limited local matches (such as observing a single feature) can be unambiguous. However, if the robot's positional estimate is highly uncertain, the same match is no longer sufficient." — [Olson 2009 RAS](https://april.eecs.umich.edu/media/pdfs/olson2009ras.pdf)
- Degeneracy handling: Zhang, Kaess, Singh "determine and separate degenerate directions in the state space, and only partially solve the problem in well-conditioned directions" (eigen-analysis of the problem's constraint matrix). — [Zhang et al. ICRA 2016](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1109/ICRA.2016.7487211)
- Censi: closed-form ICP covariance from the Hessian of the error function, accounting for correlated correspondences. — [Censi ICRA 2007 (project page)](https://censi.science/research/robot-perception/icpcov/)

### Inferences
- Why the doorframe match was likely rejected: a sparse 72-bin sonar sweep near a doorframe is dominated by two parallel walls, so the score surface is a ridge along the wall direction; a runner-up 1-2 cells away (or one door-width away) scores nearly the same, so best/runner-up ratio is ~1 even when the heading and cross-wall position are well determined.
- Replace best/runner-up with: (1) score-surface covariance (Olson formula, using exp(score/T) as weights over the evaluated grid); (2) accept per-axis: if heading sigma and cross-track sigma are small, apply the heading and cross-track correction and keep along-track from odometry (Zhang-style partial solve); (3) non-maximum suppression for ambiguity: runner-up = best score outside a radius of ~2-3 cells and ~5 deg from the peak; reject only if a distant peak is close in score AND lies inside the plausible region (prior), i.e. Olson's global sufficiency test with the current covariance.
- Combine prior and likelihood before computing confidence (as Karto's distance/angle penalties and Cartographer's delta-cost weights do). A secondary peak that requires an implausible jump given odometry should not veto the primary peak.
- Absolute score floors should be relative to the sweep: normalize by the number of valid (non-max-range) readings; with sonar dropouts, a fixed fraction threshold like Cartographer's 0.55 is a starting point, not a validated value.

### Gaps
- No published thresholds found for sonar-specific correlative matching confidence; Karto/Cartographer numbers are lidar-tuned.
- Karto's DISTANCE_PENALTY_GAIN / ANGLE_PENALTY_GAIN constant values were not confirmed (defined in a header not retrieved).

## 4. Local submaps vs whole drifted map; keyframes; scan-to-scan vs scan-to-map; lightweight loop closure

### Takeaway
Match against a recent local submap for odometry-like tracking, and treat revisits of older areas as loop closures (scan-to-old-submap with a large window and strict acceptance), resolved with a small pose graph. With sparse sonar, a single sweep is weak; accumulating several sweeps into a local submap/keyframe gives much more matchable structure.

### Cited Findings
- Cartographer: local matching "happens against a recent submap, so it only depends on recent scans, and the error of pose estimates in the world frame accumulates"; finished submaps are used for loop closure; "If a sufficiently good match is found in a search window around the currently estimated pose, it is added as a loop closing constraint"; optimization every few seconds (Sparse Pose Adjustment). Default submap size num_range_data = 90. — [Hess et al. 2016](https://research.google.com/pubs/archive/45466.pdf); [trajectory_builder_2d.lua](https://github.com/cartographer-project/cartographer/blob/master/configuration_files/trajectory_builder_2d.lua)
- Cartographer loop closure only considers submaps within max_constraint_distance 15 m and samples sampling_ratio 0.3 of candidates; loop closure weights 1.1e4 (translation) and 1e5 (rotation); huber_scale 10. — [pose_graph.lua](https://github.com/cartographer-project/cartographer/blob/master/configuration_files/pose_graph.lua)
- Karto/slam_toolbox: sequential matching against a buffer of recent scans (scan_buffer_size 10, scan_buffer_maximum_scan_distance 10 m); near "chains" matched and linked if within link_scan_maximum_distance 1.5 m; loop search within loop_search_maximum_distance 3 m. — [mapper_params_online_async.yaml](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/blob/ros2/config/mapper_params_online_async.yaml); [Karto Mapper.cpp](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/blob/ros2/lib/karto_sdk/src/Mapper.cpp)
- Sonar data is sparse: Polaroid-class sonar gives about 50 readings per second; other studies needed "more than 10 seconds ... to gather enough sonar data to perform localization"; Burguera et al. therefore match against a history of readings accumulated over the trajectory. — [Burguera et al., Sensors 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3267219/)
- Sonar scan matching frameworks (sICP, sIDC, spIC, likelihood-field sonar matching) exist; spIC models sparseness and noise and was reported more robust than sICP/sIDC under noisy readings and large odometric errors. — [Burguera et al. ICRA 2007](https://dspace.uib.es/xmlui/bitstream/handle/11201/172118/137010_ICRA2007_ACCEPTAT.pdf?sequence=1&isAllowed=y); [Burguera et al., Autonomous Robots (likelihood fields for sonar)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10514-009-9108-0)
- Tardós et al. used local maps, perceptual grouping (Hough transform) of sonar returns into lines and corners, and robust data association for sonar SLAM. — [Tardós et al. IJRR 2002](https://researchgate.net/publication/2495107_Robust_Mapping_and_Localization_in_Indoor_Environments_Using_Sonar_Data)
- Robust pose graph back ends: switchable constraints add a switch variable per loop closure, letting the optimizer disable outliers; handled up to 1000 false loop closures in their evaluation. — [Sünderhauf & Protzel IROS 2012](https://nikosuenderhauf.github.io/assets/papers/IROS12-switchableConstraints.pdf)

### Inferences
- Lightweight design for the browser: store each accepted sweep as a keyframe (pose + 72 endpoints). Keep a local submap = last N keyframes (e.g. 5-10, or last ~1-2 m of travel) rendered into its own likelihood field; match new sweeps to it. When the predicted pose comes within ~1 m of an older keyframe from a different "session leg", attempt a loop-closure match against a likelihood field built from those old keyframes only, with a wide window and strict acceptance.
- On an accepted loop closure, run a tiny 2D pose-graph relaxation (Gauss-Newton or even linear interpolation of the correction along the chain of keyframes since the last anchor) and re-rasterize the grid from keyframes. This is what removes duplicated obstacles retroactively; with only tens to hundreds of keyframes, a dense or simple iterative solver in JS is adequate.
- Matching a drifted sweep against the whole global grid is fine if the global grid is consistent; the problem is that a grid already corrupted by mis-registered sweeps contains duplicate walls, which create competing peaks. Keeping keyframes (not just the fused grid) allows rebuilding the grid after correction.

### Gaps
- No source found for a minimal JS pose-graph library; implementation detail left to engineering.

## 5. Robust estimation: truncated costs, RANSAC, dynamic/new obstacles

### Takeaway
Likelihood-field scoring is already a bounded (truncated) cost: each reading contributes at most a fixed max, so new or false readings cannot dominate. Keep that property explicitly (cap per-point contribution, add a uniform floor) and avoid unbounded squared-distance costs. RANSAC/consensus is useful for sparse sonar when converting to line features.

### Cited Findings
- Likelihood-field / lookup-table models approximate each reading's probability by its distance to the nearest surface, ignoring visibility. — [Olson ICRA 2009](https://april.eecs.umich.edu/pdfs/olson2009icra.pdf)
- AMCL likelihood_field defaults (Nav2): z_hit 0.5, z_rand 0.5, sigma_hit 0.2 m, laser_likelihood_max_dist 2.0 m, max_beams 60; beam skipping (off by default): beam_skip_distance 0.5, beam_skip_threshold 0.3, beam_skip_error_threshold 0.9. — [nav2_params.yaml](https://github.com/ros-navigation/navigation2/blob/main/nav2_bringup/params/nav2_params.yaml)
- Cartographer pose graph uses a Huber loss to "reduce the influence of outliers which can appear ... when scan matching adds incorrect constraints." — [Hess et al. 2016](https://research.google.com/pubs/archive/45466.pdf)
- Burguera et al. model each sonar reading as a 2D Gaussian whose cross-range spread grows with range and sensor opening, and gate correspondences with a chi-squared test on Mahalanobis distance (2 DOF). — [Burguera et al., Sensors 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3267219/)
- Measured sonar maximum angle of incidence at 1 m: 81 deg for a rough stone wall but only 16 deg for a flat wooden panel; opening varies with range. — [Burguera et al., Sensors 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3267219/)
- Olson's Single Cluster Graph Partitioning finds the most mutually consistent subset of match hypotheses (dominant eigenvector of the pairwise consistency matrix), rejecting outliers. — [Olson 2009 RAS](https://april.eecs.umich.edu/media/pdfs/olson2009ras.pdf)

### Inferences
- For sonar, use a wide sigma in the likelihood field (beam footprint at 1 m with a 25-30 deg beam is roughly 45-55 cm across; range accuracy is much better), or better, an anisotropic model: tight in range, wide in bearing (Burguera). Practical JS approximation: smear the field with sigma ~ 8-10 cm (range) and accept that bearing error is absorbed by the 5 deg resampling and search.
- Score = sum over valid readings of max(log p(d), floor), with max-range/no-echo readings excluded (specular dropouts are not evidence of free space). Equivalent to AMCL's z_hit + z_rand mixture.
- New obstacles (moved chair, person) only lower a few readings' scores and cannot pull the match if contributions are capped; it is when static structure is under-represented (few valid returns) that matches go wrong, so count valid readings and refuse to match below a minimum (e.g. < ~20 valid of 72; to be tuned).
- RANSAC/Hough line extraction over the last few sweeps gives wall-segment features that are robust to individual dropouts (Tardós approach) and a natural way to detect corridor degeneracy (all lines parallel).

### Gaps
- No quantitative sonar-specific results found for truncated likelihood fields vs Mahalanobis-gated ICP in this low-cost (HC-SR04/mBot class) sensor setting.

## 6. Occupancy evidence as a prior; weighting by cell confidence; how ROS stacks parameterize this

### Takeaway
Use only confirmed cells to build the matching likelihood field, and treat suspect/new cells as low weight; this both stabilizes matching and prevents duplicates from becoming self-reinforcing. ROS stacks expose this through occupancy thresholds, hit/miss probabilities, and odometry penalties.

### Cited Findings
- Cartographer match score uses the probability grid value at each projected point (Ceres local matcher minimizes sum (1 - M_smooth(T h_k))^2 with bicubic interpolation); weights: occupied_space_weight 1, translation_weight 10, rotation_weight 40 (local), described as "a static covariance". — [Hess et al. 2016](https://research.google.com/pubs/archive/45466.pdf); [tuning docs](https://google-cartographer-ros.readthedocs.io/en/latest/algo_walkthrough.html); [trajectory_builder_2d.lua](https://github.com/cartographer-project/cartographer/blob/master/configuration_files/trajectory_builder_2d.lua)
- slam_toolbox: occupancy_threshold 0.1, min_pass_through 2 (rasterization), correlation smear deviation 0.1 m, distance_variance_penalty 0.5, angle_variance_penalty 1.0, minimum_distance_penalty 0.5, minimum_angle_penalty 0.9. Karto multiplies each candidate's response by (1 - k d^2 / distance_variance_penalty) and (1 - k dtheta^2 / angle_variance_penalty), floored at the minimum penalties. — [mapper_params_online_async.yaml](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/blob/ros2/config/mapper_params_online_async.yaml); [Karto Mapper.cpp](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/blob/ros2/lib/karto_sdk/src/Mapper.cpp)
- GMapping: improved proposal samples around the scan-matched mode combined with odometry; adaptive resampling when N_eff < N/2. — [Grisetti et al. 2007](http://www2.informatik.uni-freiburg.de/~stachnis/pdf/grisetti07tro.pdf)

### Inferences
- Build the likelihood field from cells with log-odds above a "confirmed" threshold (e.g. hit count >= 2-3 from different poses and P(occ) > ~0.7); include "suspect" cells (single hit) at reduced weight or not at all. This prevents a duplicate doorframe created by one bad sweep from attracting future matches.
- Add Karto-style odometry penalties with floors (e.g. 0.5 for distance, 0.9 for angle) so the prior breaks ties without overwhelming strong evidence.
- Use weak per-sweep evidence (Cartographer-like 0.55/0.49) so that a single mis-registered sweep cannot flip a cell to confirmed; sonar's wide beam argues for even weaker hit updates spread across the arc.

### Gaps
- No ROS/Cartographer documentation found that explicitly weights matching by per-cell confirmation count; this recommendation is an inference from how their probability grids feed the score.
